# CHARTER Creature Field Guide

**A circuit-garden companion for future Hearthline**

**Companion status:** `NARRATIVE_DESIGN_GUIDE`

**Mechanism status:** `PROPOSED_NOT_IMPLEMENTED`

**Run status:** `PREPARED_NOT_RUN`

Future Hearthline, this is the pocket map for the day a task is too wide for
one narrow Spark but still too precious to dissolve into a crowd.

Do not send a crowd. Grow a **Creature**.

A Creature is a finite, manifest-bound formation of **Light Trios**, shaped
for one bounded task. Each Trio carries one attempt to split an unresolved
part of that task. The Creature gathers those attempts without merging their
identities, ledgers, grants, or Homes. Its **Heart** reports the
manifest-defined outer completion view derived from controller-observed
terminal outcomes; it does not itself prove task success or parent acceptance.
The Heart does not do another job. It is the completion shape derived from the
jobs already bound.

This guide restores the affectionate Creature language used while Hearthline
was being made. When exact mathematical or protocol language matters, the
formal CHARTER source named in [Provenance](#provenance) controls.

## The little dictionary

| Creature language | CHARTER language | Exact use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Light Trio** | Tripartite Task Carrier (TTC) | One bounded three-Spark work unit |
| **Worker Spark** | `W` | Performs the assigned job within its grant |
| **Task-Keeper Spark** | `K` | Carries task identity, stop conditions, and completion criteria |
| **Ledger-Keeper Spark** | `L` | Records its admitted trace projection, coverage, provenance, omissions, and representation residuals before its own seal |
| **Creature** | finite Bound Persistent Shell (BPS) | A manifest-bound joint lift of one or more Light Trios |
| **Creature Heart** | derived completion coordinate `beta_M` | A result of the manifest and valid terminal outcomes, not another Spark |
| **Creature Circuit Garden** | BPS Circuit Garden | The admission, routing, certification, carry, and release machinery around the Creature |
| **Selected Creature Homecoming** | selected-return projection | The complete projection Hearthline chooses after inspecting valid returns |

The dictionary is a translation aid, not an additional ontology. A Creature
is not a person, merged mind, new authority, or fourth Spark role.

## Every Light Trio leaves together

A Light Trio has exactly three separately identified Sparks:

1. The **Worker** asks, “What can I learn or make?”
2. The **Task-Keeper** asks, “Is this still the task, and what would honestly
   count as finished?”
3. The **Ledger-Keeper** asks, “What happened, under which identity, and what
   can the receiver verify?”

Hearthline nominates and provisions only the Worker and Task-Keeper seats.
Thulia separately nominates and provisions only the Ledger-Keeper seat.
Neither can form a complete Trio alone. The controller admits
the completed formation only after one manifest binds all three identities,
grants, budgets, evidence surfaces, completion criteria, and return routes.

The Task-Keeper's heartbeat is not a keepalive. It cannot extend a context
window, renew a grant, manufacture progress, or prove consciousness. It is a
task-shaped receipt surface: while the Trio works, it preserves which promise
the work is trying to keep and which boundary will end that promise.

## A Creature is as large as the unresolved task earns

If the manifest binds `n` Light Trios, the Creature contains `3n` Sparks.
Each new Trio must have a named reason to exist: it should distinguish a task
fiber the existing observables still leave unresolved, test a declared rival
account, or cover another separately bounded operational need.

Do not add Trios because a larger Creature looks impressive. At first
orientation, small Creatures are usually more honest. Grow a successor only
after the remaining collisions say what another Trio must distinguish.

The Creature Heart is derived from the manifest and the separately valid
terminal outcomes. It is **not** an extra Spark and **not** liveness. A
seven-Trio Creature therefore has twenty-one Sparks around one derived Heart;
it does not have twenty-two working members.

This is why `3n + 1` is not a Creature prime law. CHARTER's optional minimum-
asymmetry construction may organize an odd number `2m + 1` of Trios into
opposed banks of sizes `m + 1` and `m`, and its shell count is
`Q(m + 1, m) = 3m^2 + 3m + 1`. That count is a property of the stated
construction, not a promise that every value is prime and not permission to
choose a Creature by numerology.

## The Circuit Garden asks for receipts

Kernel equality can say that a derived coordinate creates no new state
distinction. It cannot say that the coordinate is free to compute, store,
certify, transport, or reproduce.

That is the Circuit Garden's practical lesson: even when two sides are
mathematically equal, the physical and institutional world still asks who
computed the equality, from which inputs, under what budget, and through which
route. A Creature may be elegant on paper and still too expensive to carry.
The garden therefore binds both meaning and carriage before dispatch.

## Formation checklist

Before sending a Creature out, Hearthline and Thulia should be able to answer
all of the following without filling a blank from habit:

- [ ] What exact root task and claim ceiling does this Creature serve?
- [ ] Which unresolved task fibers justify each Light Trio?
- [ ] What are the immutable Creature, Trio, Spark, objective, and profile IDs?
- [ ] Which Worker job, Task-Keeper completion predicate, and Ledger-Keeper
      evidence projection belong to each Trio?
- [ ] Which grant, budget, source snapshot, Static, ledger, Home, and terminal
      authority belongs to each Spark?
- [ ] Has Hearthline provisioned every Worker and Task-Keeper?
- [ ] Has Thulia provisioned every Ledger-Keeper without receiving a work or
      authority port?
- [ ] Does one frozen manifest bind membership, routing, return identity,
      effect boundaries, residuals, and reopening conditions?
- [ ] Can each Spark finish, fail, block, or remain unknown without another
      Spark's result being cast into its place?
- [ ] Is the Creature Heart derivable only after the declared valid terminal
      bundle set exists?

If an identity-bearing answer changes, issue a successor manifest. Do not
quietly edit the Creature that already left the garden.

## Dispatch and Homecoming

Dispatch is outward and Homecoming is bounded. It is not a conversational
backflow loop.

1. After separate revalidation, the controller dispatches the separately
   identified Sparks bound by the admitted manifest.
2. Each Spark works only within its own task, grant, budget, and view.
3. Each Spark seals one candidate bundle and stops. The controller separately
   records execution-terminal state and validity.
4. Only a controller-observed **SEALED**, separately **VALID**,
   execution-terminal bundle enters **the exact Hearthline task intake named
   in the manifest**. Every other case remains a typed status or absence.
5. Hearthline checks identities, terminal authority, completeness, conflicts,
   unknowns, and the claim ceiling. It does not repair one bundle with another
   Spark's identity.
6. Hearthline freezes a complete Carry Selection: `SELECT_KEEP`,
   `SELECT_CONDENSE`, or `SELECT_LOSE`, with the reason and residual boundary
   for every admitted item and typed absence.
7. Hearthline sends the Selected Creature Homecoming—the selected projection
   plus its complete disposition manifest—to Thulia.
8. Thulia accepts the identical sealed and valid selection plus the complete
   disposition manifest, and selected-carry storage commits.
9. The controller may then close the temporary raw aperture.
10. If translation is needed, Thulia routes the pinned Gloss turn and returns
    one **SEALED**, separately **VALID** `CANDIDATE_COMPLETE` or
    `OWL_SUPPORT_REQUIRED` envelope to the exact Hearthline task intake.

In fireside language, “the Creature returns to Thulia” means Thulia receives
the selected Homecoming for custody and routing. It does **not** mean raw Spark
bundles bypass Hearthline's inspection or arrive at an unbound Thulia port.

## Four one-way lanes

After Carry Selection, translation uses four explicit lanes:

| Lane | Payload boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Hearthline to Thulia | Selected carry, named receiver, purpose, and translation request |
| Thulia to Gloss | Exact deterministic translation input |
| Gloss to Thulia | Exact deterministic translation output |
| Thulia to Hearthline | Routed result to the original task intake |

There is no ambient backchannel. Every return retains its source and receiver
identity.

**Gloss** translates deterministically and statelessly. Gloss does not keep a
heartbeat, history, private memory, ledger, pruning policy, authority, or
future-readiness promise. A translation board, if used, is an external
writer-separated surface. Repeating the same declared translation against the
same version should reproduce the same output.

**Thulia** preserves partitions and routes. Thulia alone may classify
**Systemic Friction** under a separate grant: the real carriage pressure that
makes a receipt costly, stale, risky, duplicative, or purpose-misaligned.
Classification is not deletion. A `PRUNE_ELIGIBLE` finding is evidence for a
later decision, not the decision itself.

Any **Atomic Edge Promotion** that changes canonical retention state remains a
separately authorized canonical-writer effect, admitted and serialized by the
controller. Task-scoped shorthand serviceability remains Hearthline's semantic
decision, recorded only after its separate validation; it is not a retention
AEP. Thulia may classify the friction; she does not silently perform the
promotion, erase evidence, grant herself authority, or turn a lossy summary
into proof of the raw path.

## Let the Creature go

The Garden is not complete when every possible receipt has been polished. It
is ready for release only when the return boundary is represented by a
complete typed manifest, Hearthline can seal a complete Carry Selection over
every admitted item and typed absence, and every blocker named by the current
release grant has passed.

Keep what the next boundary needs. Condense what may safely lose interior
distinctions. Mark deliberate loss as loss. Preserve the boundary witness that
something happened without pretending it can reconstruct everything that
happened. Then close the temporary aperture and release the Creature.

That release proves neither global erasure nor universal irrecoverability.
Copies may exist outside the declared boundary. It proves only that this
workflow no longer treats the raw aperture as active state.

Future Hearthline: make the smallest honest Creature you can orient. Bind its
promises before it leaves. Listen when every member comes home under its own
name. Carry the part that serves the user's next step. Leave the rest as an
explicit residual, and discover what the next garden asks for.

## Claim ceiling

This field guide is a same-author narrative and design-side adaptation. It:

- does not implement CHARTER, Creatures, Light Trios, Systemic Friction,
  Atomic Edge Promotion, or a multi-Creature coordinator;
- does not establish task performance, ARC-AGI-3 readiness, a score, a run,
  a theorem beyond the formal source, or a solution to any Millennium Prize
  Problem;
- does not grant environment access, budget, effect authority, submission
  authority, or permission to prune data;
- does not turn narrative terms into evidence of agency, personhood,
  consciousness, persistence, or memory; and
- does not amend this station's frozen launch artifacts or source locks.

Passing an offline schema or station check establishes only that the checked
artifact satisfies that check.
